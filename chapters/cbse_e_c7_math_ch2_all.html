Chapter Summary

▼

What is an Arithmetic Expression?

An arithmetic expression is a combination of numbers and arithmetic operations (+, −, ×, ÷) that represents a value. Expressions can be simple like 3 + 5 or complex like 4 × 9 + 2 × 6 − 8 ÷ 4.

Expression

A mathematical phrase using numbers and operations. Has a value but no "=" sign alone.

E.g.: 5 × 23 + 5

Terms

Parts of an expression separated by + or −. Multiplication/division groups form ONE term.

E.g.: 5 × 23 and 5 are two terms.

Equality (=)

Two expressions are equal when they evaluate to the same number.

E.g.: 13 + 4 = 11 + 6 = 17

BODMAS Order

The rule for evaluating mixed expressions correctly.

Brackets → Orders → ÷ → × → + → −

BODMAS Rule — Order of Operations

B
Brackets
O
Orders (powers)
D
Division
M
Multiplication
A
Addition
S
Subtraction
Division and Multiplication have equal priority — solve left to right. Same for Addition and Subtraction.

Subtraction = Adding a Negative Number

a − b = a + (−b)

Example: 83 − 14 = 83 + (−14) = 69. This lets us treat every expression as a sum of terms (some positive, some negative).

Properties of Addition in Expressions

Commutative Property

Order of terms doesn't matter for addition.

a + b = b + a

E.g.: (−3) + 7 = 7 + (−3) = 4

Associative Property

Grouping of terms doesn't matter for addition.

(a+b)+c = a+(b+c)

E.g.: {(−2)+(−5)}+(−7) = −14

Distributive Property

a × (b + c) = a × b + a × c

Example: 3 × (4 + 5) = 3 × 4 + 3 × 5 = 12 + 15 = 27

Key Takeaways

  • An expression does not require a = sign; it is evaluated to get a value.
  • When expressions are equal in value, we connect them with =.
  • Brackets change the order of evaluation — always solve brackets first.
  • Subtracting b is the same as adding (−b); so every expression with subtraction can be written as a sum of terms.
  • Terms of an expression can be added in any order due to commutative and associative properties.
  • When comparing two expressions, look for common parts and only compare the differences.

Keywords & Important Points

▼
Arithmetic Expression
A combination of numbers and operations (+, −, ×, ÷) that has a value.
Term
A single number or product/quotient of numbers in an expression, separated by + or −.
Value of Expression
The single number you get after evaluating all operations.
Equality (=)
Two expressions are equal when they have the same value.
BODMAS
Rule for order: Brackets, Orders, Division, Multiplication, Addition, Subtraction.
Brackets
(), {}, [] — force a part of an expression to be calculated first.
Additive Inverse
The negative of a number. The additive inverse of 5 is −5.
Commutative Property
a + b = b + a. Changing order does NOT change sum.
Associative Property
(a+b)+c = a+(b+c). Changing grouping does NOT change sum.
Distributive Property
a × (b+c) = a×b + a×c. Multiplication distributes over addition.
Token Model
Visual model using positive (+) and negative (−) tokens to show integer operations.
Comparing Expressions
Simplify by removing common terms from both sides, then compare the remainder.

⚡ Important Points at a Glance

  • Subtraction as addition: a − b = a + (−b). Every expression is really a sum of terms.
  • Multiplication/Division before Addition/Subtraction (unless brackets change the order).
  • Brackets matter: 3 + 4 × 2 = 11 but (3 + 4) × 2 = 14. Big difference!
  • Comparing trick: For 245 + 289 vs 246 + 285 — cancel the common part (246 + 285), then compare what's left: 289−285=4 vs 246−245=1, so left side is bigger.
  • Remainder form: If 33 children play in groups of 5 → expression is 6 × 5 + 3. Terms: (6×5) and 3.
  • Sign rules in multiplication: (−) × (+) = negative; (−) × (−) = positive.
  • Commutativity is NOT valid for subtraction: 8 − 3 ≠ 3 − 8.

Q Verify: subtracting a number = adding its inverse. Give examples. ▼
✅ SOLUTION
17 − 9 = 17 + (−9) = 8 ✓
8 − 5 = 8 + (−5) = 3 ✓
12 − 4 + 6 × 3 = 12 + (−4) + 18 = 26 ✓
Rule: a − b = a + (−b). The minus sign "belongs" to the term that follows it.
Page 29 — Commutativity of Addition
Q Does changing the order of terms in addition change the value? Does it hold for negative numbers too? ▼
✅ SOLUTION

No, changing the order does NOT change the value.

Example 1: (−3) + (−9) = −12 and (−9) + (−3) = −12 ✓
Example 2: (−5) + 7 = 2 and 7 + (−5) = 2 ✓
This is the Commutative Property of Addition: a + b = b + a, works for all integers.
Page 30 — Associativity of Addition
Q Does changing the grouping of terms in addition change the value? Show with negative numbers. ▼
✅ SOLUTION

No, grouping doesn't change the value (Associative Property).

LHS: {(−2)+(−5)}+(−7) = (−7)+(−7) = −14
RHS: (−2)+{(−5)+(−7)} = (−2)+(−12) = −14 ✓
Associative Property: (a+b)+c = a+(b+c)

✅ SOLUTION

Correct total = 11,749 + 9,055 = 20,804
Answer: 20,804

Q "Fire in the Mountain": 33 students, teacher calls '5'. Ruby writes 6×5+3. Why? ▼
✅ SOLUTION

33 ÷ 5 = 6 (quotient) remainder 3.

6 complete groups of 5 formed → 6 × 5 = 30 students
3 students left over who couldn't form a group → + 3
Expression: 6 × 5 + 3 = 33. Terms: (6×5) and 3
