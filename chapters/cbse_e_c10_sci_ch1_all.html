Comprehensive Definitions & Explanations

1. Fundamentals of Chemical Reactions & Equations

  • Chemical Reaction: A process in which chemical bonds break and reform, transforming starting materials into entirely new chemical substances with distinct chemical identities and properties.
  • Reactants: The initial molecular or ionic substances that actively change composition during a chemical reaction, written on the Left-Hand Side (LHS) of an equation.
  • Products: The newly arranged atomic chemical compounds generated by a chemical reaction, displayed on the Right-Hand Side (RHS) of an equation.
  • Word Equation: A basic representation of a chemical reaction using literal substance names separated by an addition sign and reaction direction arrow.
  • Chemical Equation: A symbolic and shorthand representation of a chemical reaction using chemical formulas, structural coefficients, and state symbols.
  • Skeletal Chemical Equation: An unbalanced equation where the elemental atom counts do not match on both sides, serving as a raw qualitative statement.
  • Balanced Chemical Equation: A complete equation where each element contains an identical atom count on both sides, ensuring compliance with physical conservation metrics.
  • Law of Conservation of Mass: A physical chemistry law stating matter cannot be generated or destroyed inside isolated chemical updates; total initial mass must equal final product mass.
  • Hit-and-Trial Method: An algebraic inspection technique used to balance skeletal chemical equations by varying coefficients without shifting base subscripts.
  • Physical States Notation: Informative notations explicitly marked as (s) for solid, (l) for liquid, (g) for gas, and (aq) for aqueous systems dissolved in water.

2. Types of Chemical Reactions

  • Combination Reaction: A synthesis pathway where two or more fundamental chemical elements or small compounds merge into a single compound product.
  • Exothermic Chemical Reaction: A chemical step that generates and radiates thermal energy out into the immediate test surroundings.
  • Decomposition Reaction: A reaction pattern where a single complex reactant splits into two or more clean elements or simpler molecular fragments.
  • Thermal Decomposition: A decomposition process driven by continuous heat input.
  • Electrolytic Decomposition (Electrolysis): A decomposition process driven by passing an external electric current through an electrolyte.
  • Photolytic Decomposition (Photolysis): A clean degradation pathway driven by photons from light or direct sunlight.
  • Endothermic Reaction: A reaction network that requires continuous energy absorption from external systems to break its molecular bonds.
  • Displacement Reaction: A single replacement reaction where a metal with higher thermodynamic reactivity displaces a less reactive metal ion from its salt solution.
  • Double Displacement Reaction: A reaction process where two ionic compounds swap structural partners to generate two new products.
  • Precipitate: An insoluble solid compound that drops out of liquid phases during aqueous ionic updates.
  • Precipitation Reaction: An aqueous mixing sequence that directly produces an insoluble solid precipitate.

3. Oxidation-Reduction & Practical Degradation

  • Oxidation: A process defined by the gain of oxygen atoms or the loss of hydrogen atoms from an atomic or molecular substance.
  • Reduction: A process defined by the absolute loss of oxygen atoms or the gain of hydrogen atoms by a substance.
  • Redox Reaction: A coupled reaction system where oxidation and reduction steps happen simultaneously.
  • Oxidizing Agent: A chemical component that provides oxygen or accepts hydrogen, causing another target substance to oxidize.
  • Reducing Agent: A compound that extracts oxygen or provides hydrogen, causing another target substance to reduce.
  • Corrosion: The slow conversion of metal structures into oxides, carbonates, or sulphides through exposure to moist air and chemical gases.
  • Rusting: The specific corrosion path of iron forming flaky, hydrated reddish-brown iron oxide layers on exposure to moist air.
  • Rancidity: The air oxidation of lipid and fat chains inside open food goods, spoiling their natural flavor and aroma.
  • Antioxidants: Sacrificial compounds mixed into commercial food packets to neutralize oxygen fractions and check rancidification.

Detailed Analysis of All 11 Chapter Activities

This section explores every practical experiment documented within the textbook blueprint in sequential order with high-quality experimental setups.

Activity 1.1: Combustion of Magnesium Ribbon

Procedure: Clean a 3-4 cm long magnesium ribbon with sandpaper, hold it using a pair of tongs, and burn it over a spirit lamp while collecting the ash in a watch-glass.

Observation: The magnesium ribbon burns with a dazzling white flame, changing into a fine white powder.

Inference & Chemical Equation: Magnesium undergoes a combination reaction with atmospheric oxygen to create magnesium oxide.

2Mg(s) + O2(g) → 2MgO(s)

Activity 1.2: Reaction of Lead Nitrate with Potassium Iodide

Procedure: Mix lead nitrate solution with potassium iodide solution in a laboratory test tube.

2) Potassium iodide solution

Observation: A bright, instantaneous yellow precipitate settles out of the solution.

Inference & Chemical Equation: This represents a double displacement and precipitation reaction where insoluble lead iodide separates.

Pb(NO3)2(aq) + 2KI(aq) → PbI2(s)↓ + 2KNO3(aq)

Activity 1.3: Reaction of Zinc Granules with Dilute Acid

Procedure: Put zinc granules into a conical flask and slowly introduce dilute hydrochloric acid or dilute sulphuric acid.

Observation: Gas bubbles evolve rapidly around the zinc pieces, and the flask becomes hot to the touch.

Inference & Chemical Equation: An exothermic single displacement reaction occurs, releasing hydrogen gas.

Zn(s) + H2SO4(aq) → ZnSO4(aq) + H2(g)↑

Activity 1.4: Reaction of Calcium Oxide with Water

Procedure: Place a small quantity of calcium oxide (quicklime) inside a glass beaker and slowly add water.

Observation: A vigorous reaction occurs with a hissing sound, causing a sharp rise in temperature.

Inference & Chemical Equation: An highly exothermic combination reaction yields calcium hydroxide (slaked lime).

CaO(s) + H2O(l) → Ca(OH)2(aq) + Heat

Activity 1.5: Thermal Decomposition of Ferrous Sulphate

Procedure: Heat approximately 2 g of green ferrous sulphate crystals inside a dry boiling tube.

2, SO3) Green crystals turn brown Boiling tube

Observation: The green color fades to a brown solid, and pungent gases with the odor of burning sulphur escape.

Inference & Chemical Equation: Thermal decomposition breaks down the crystals into ferric oxide while releasing sulphur gases.

2FeSO4(s) + Heat → Fe2O3(s) + SO2(g) + SO3(g)

Activity 1.6: Thermal Decomposition of Lead Nitrate

Procedure: Hold a boiling tube containing 2 g of lead nitrate powder with tongs and heat it over an open burner.

2 gas) White Lead Nitrate

Observation: Thick, choking brown gas fumes escape, leaving a yellowish solid behind.

Inference & Chemical Equation: Lead nitrate undergoes thermal decomposition, yielding nitrogen dioxide gas.

2Pb(NO3)2(s) + Heat → 2PbO(s) + 4NO2(g)↑ + O2(g)

Activity 1.7: Electrolytic Decomposition of Water

Procedure: Set up carbon electrodes inside a water-filled plastic mug, invert water-filled test tubes over them, and pass a 6V direct current.

Observation: Gas bubbles accumulate at both electrodes, but the volume of gas collected at the cathode is exactly twice that at the anode.

Inference & Chemical Equation: Water undergoes electrolytic decomposition, splitting into hydrogen and oxygen in a 2:1 ratio by volume.

2H2O(l) + Electrical Current → 2H2(g)↑ [Cathode] + O2(g)↑ [Anode]

Activity 1.8: Photolytic Decomposition of Silver Chloride

Procedure: Place 2 g of white silver chloride powder in an open china dish under direct sunlight.

Observation: The white powder turns a dull grey color after exposure.

Inference & Chemical Equation: Ambient light causes photolytic decomposition, splitting the compound into silver metal and chlorine gas.

2AgCl(s) + Sunlight → 2Ag(s) + Cl2(g)↑

Activity 1.9: Displacement Reaction of Iron Nails in Copper Sulphate

Procedure: Suspend clean iron nails into a test tube filled with blue copper sulphate solution for 20 minutes.

4 Green FeSO4 Brown Copper Coating

Observation: The iron nails acquire a brown coating, and the blue color of the solution fades to light green.

Inference & Chemical Equation: Iron is more reactive than copper and displaces it from the salt solution.

Fe(s) + CuSO4(aq) → FeSO4(aq) + Cu(s)

Activity 1.10: Double Displacement of Sodium Sulphate and Barium Chloride

Procedure: Mix 3 mL of sodium sulphate solution with 3 mL of barium chloride solution in a test tube.

4)

Observation: An instantaneous white solid precipitate forms throughout the mixed solution.

Inference & Chemical Equation: A double displacement reaction occurs via the mutual exchange of Ba2+ and SO42- ions.

Na2SO4(aq) + BaCl2(aq) → BaSO4(s)↓ + 2NaCl(aq)

Activity 1.11: Oxidation of Copper Powder

Procedure: Heat about 1 g of brown copper powder inside a china dish over a laboratory burner.

Observation: The surface of the brown copper powder turns black.

Inference & Chemical Equation: Copper binds with oxygen in an oxidation process to form black copper(II) oxide.

2Cu(s) + O2(g) + Heat → 2CuO(s)

PART I: SOLUTIONS TO QUESTIONS INSIDE THE CHAPTER

In-Text Questions (Page 6)

Q1: Why should a magnesium ribbon be cleaned before burning in air?

Answer: Magnesium reacts with atmospheric oxygen to form a thin, stable protective coat of basic magnesium carbonate on its surface. This film acts as a shield and arrests smooth flame propagation during heating. Rubbing with sandpaper strips away this passive layer to expose the pure reactive metal beneath.

Q2: Write the balanced equation for the following chemical reactions:
(i) Hydrogen + Chlorine → Hydrogen chloride
(ii) Barium chloride + Aluminium sulphate → Barium sulphate + Aluminium chloride
(iii) Sodium + Water → Sodium hydroxide + Hydrogen

Answer:
(i) H2(g) + Cl2(g) → 2HCl(g)
(ii) 3BaCl2(aq) + Al2(SO4)3(aq) → 3BaSO4(s)↓ + 2AlCl3(aq)
(iii) 2Na(s) + 2H2O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H2(g)↑

Q3: Write a balanced chemical equation with state symbols for the following reactions:
(i) Solutions of barium chloride and sodium sulphate in water react to give insoluble barium sulphate and the solution of sodium chloride.
(ii) Sodium hydroxide solution (in water) reacts with hydrochloric acid solution (in water) to produce sodium chloride solution and water.

Answer:
(i) BaCl2(aq) + Na2SO4(aq) → BaSO4(s)↓ + 2NaCl(aq)
(ii) NaOH(aq) + HCl(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H2}O(l)

In-Text Questions (Page 10)

Q1: A solution of a substance 'X' is used for whitewashing.
(i) Name the substance 'X' and write its formula.
(ii) Write the reaction of the substance 'X' named in (i) above with water.

Answer:
(i) Substance 'X' is Calcium Oxide (commonly termed quicklime). Its chemical formula is CaO.
(ii) CaO(s) + H2O(l) → Ca(OH)2(aq)

Q2: Why is the amount of gas collected in one of the test tubes in Activity 1.7 double of the amount collected in the other? Name this gas.

Answer: The stoichiometry of water splitting requires two parts of hydrogen gas for every single part of oxygen gas evolved. Hence, the gas collected in twice the volume is Hydrogen (H2).

In-Text Questions (Page 13)

Q1: Why does the colour of copper sulphate solution change when an iron nail is dipped in it?

Answer: Iron has a higher chemical reactivity than copper. When introduced into blue copper sulphate solution, iron displaces copper ions to generate a light-green iron sulphate matrix while metallic copper settles on the nail face.

Q2: Give an example of a double displacement reaction other than the one given in Activity 1.10.

Answer: Lead nitrate solution reacts cleanly with potassium iodide fluid to swap ionic partners:
Pb(NO3)2(aq) + 2KI(aq) → PbI2(s)↓ + 2KNO3(aq)

Q3: Identify the substances that are oxidised and the substances that are reduced in the following reactions:
(i) 4Na(s) + O2(g) → 2Na2O(s)
(ii) CuO(s) + H2(g) → Cu(s) + H2O(l)

Answer:
(i) Sodium (Na) is **oxidised** (gains oxygen); Oxygen (O2) acts as the oxidizer and is **reduced**.
(ii) Hydrogen (H2) is **oxidised** into water; Copper Oxide (CuO) loses its oxygen component and is therefore **reduced**.

PART II: SOLUTIONS TO EXERCISES AT THE END OF THE CHAPTER

Chapter End Exercises (Pages 14-16)

Q1: Which of the statements about the reaction below are incorrect?
2PbO(s) + C(s) → 2Pb(s) + CO2(g)
(a) Lead is getting reduced. (b) Carbon dioxide is getting oxidised. (c) Carbon is getting oxidised. (d) Lead oxide is getting reduced.

Answer: **Statements (a) and (b) are incorrect**.

Q2: Fe2O3 + 2Al → Al2O3 + 2Fe
The above reaction is an example of a: (a) combination (b) double displacement (c) decomposition (d) displacement reaction.

Answer: **(d) displacement reaction**.

Q3: What happens when dilute hydrochloric acid is added to iron filings?

Answer: **(a) Hydrogen gas and iron chloride are produced**.

Q4: What is a balanced chemical equation? Why should chemical equations be balanced?

Answer: A balanced equation contains an identical atom count for each element across both the reactant and product zones. Equations are balanced to comply with the Law of Conservation of Mass.

Q5: Translate into chemical equations and balance them:
(a) Hydrogen gas combines with nitrogen to form ammonia.
(b) Hydrogen sulphide gas burns in air to give water and sulphur dioxide.
(c) Barium chloride reacts with aluminium sulphate to give aluminium chloride and barium sulphate precipitate.
(d) Potassium metal reacts with water to give potassium hydroxide and hydrogen gas.

Answer:
(a) 3H2(g) + N2(g) → 2NH3(g)
(b) 2H2S(g) + 3O2(g) → 2H2O(l) + 2SO2(g)
(c) 3BaCl2(aq) + Al2(SO4)3(aq) → 2AlCl3(aq) + 3BaSO4(s)↓
(d) 2K(s) + 2H2O(l) → 2KOH(aq) + H2(g)↑

Q6: Balance the following chemical equations:
(a) HNO3 + Ca(OH)2 → Ca(NO3)2 + H2O
(b) NaOH + H2SO4 → Na2SO4 + H2O
(c) NaCl + AgNO3 → AgCl + NaNO3
(d) BaCl2 + H2}SO4 → BaSO4 + HCl

Answer:
(a) 2HNO3 + Ca(OH)2 → Ca(NO3)2 + 2H2O
(b) 2NaOH + H2SO4 → Na2SO4 + 2H2O
(c) NaCl + AgNO3 → AgCl↓ + NaNO3 *(Balanced)*
(d) BaCl2 + H2}SO4 → BaSO4↓ + 2HCl

Q7: Write balanced chemical equations for the following:
(a) Calcium hydroxide + Carbon dioxide → Calcium carbonate + Water
(b) Zinc + Silver nitrate → Zinc nitrate + Silver
(c) Barium chloride + Potassium sulphate → Barium sulphate + Potassium chloride
(d) Aluminium + Copper chloride → Aluminium chloride + Copper

Answer:
(a) Ca(OH)2 + CO2 → CaCO3 + H2O
(b) Zn + 2AgNO3 → Zn(NO3)2 + 2Ag
(c) BaCl2 + K2SO4} → BaSO4↓ + 2KCl
(d) 2Al + 3CuCl2 → 2AlCl3 + 3Cu

Q8: Write balanced chemical equations and identify the reaction type:
(a) Potassium bromide(aq) + Barium iodide(aq) → Potassium iodide(aq) + Barium bromide(s)
(b) Zinc carbonate(s) → Zinc oxide(s) + Carbon dioxide(g)
(c) Hydrogen(g) + Chlorine(g) → Hydrogen chloride(g)
(d) Magnesium(s) + Hydrochloric acid(aq) → Magnesium chloride(aq) + Hydrogen(g)

Answer:
(a) 2KBr(aq) + BaI2(aq) → 2KI(aq) + BaBr2(s) [Double Displacement]
(b) ZnCO3(s) → ZnO(s) + CO2(g) [Thermal Decomposition]
(c) H2(g) + Cl2(g) → 2HCl(g) [Combination]
(d) Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl2(aq) + H2(g) [Displacement]

Q9: What does one mean by exothermic and endothermic reactions? Give examples.

Answer: Exothermic reactions release energy into their surroundings (e.g., combustion of methane). Endothermic reactions absorb thermal energy to progress forward (e.g., thermal tracking of limestone).

Q10: Why is respiration considered an exothermic reaction? Explain.

Answer: In respiration, body cells break down complex glucose molecules using inspired oxygen. This metabolic process yields water, carbon dioxide, and life-supporting chemical energy, making it an exothermic reaction pathway.

Q11: Why are decomposition reactions called the opposite of combination reactions? Write equations.

Answer: Combination reactions form a single final output compound from multiple smaller elements or species. Decomposition reactions split an integrated molecular structure into multiple simpler fragments.
* Combination: 2H2(g) + O2(g) → 2H2O(l)
* Decomposition: 2H2O(l) → 2H2(g) + O2(g)

Q12: Write one equation each for decomposition reactions where energy is supplied in the form of heat, light or electricity.

Answer:
* **Heat:** CaCO3(s) → CaO(s) + CO2(g)
* **Light:** 2AgBr(s) → 2Ag(s) + Br2(g)
* **Electricity:** 2H2O(l) → 2H2(g) + O2(g)

Q13: What is the difference between displacement and double displacement reactions? Write equations.

Answer: Single displacement reactions occur when a single reactive metal displaces a less reactive companion from its solution. Double displacement steps swap ionic partners across two participating salts simultaneously.
* Displacement: Zn + CuSO4 → ZnSO4} + Cu
* Double Displacement: Na2}SO4 + BaCl2 → BaSO4↓ + 2NaCl

Q14: In the refining of silver, the recovery of silver from silver nitrate solution involved displacement by copper metal. Write down the reaction involved.

Answer: Cu(s) + 2AgNO3(aq) → Cu(NO3)2(aq) + 2Ag(s)

Q15: What do you mean by a precipitation reaction? Explain by giving examples.

Answer: A precipitation reaction is a chemical process where two clear, dissolved solutions mix to form an insoluble solid product called a precipitate. Example:
Na2SO4(aq) + BaCl2(aq) → BaSO4(s)↓ + 2NaCl(aq)

Q16: Explain the following in terms of gain or loss of oxygen with two examples each: (a) Oxidation (b) Reduction

Answer:
(a) **Oxidation:** Chemical gain of oxygen atoms. Examples: 2Mg + O2 → 2MgO and C + O2 → CO2.
(b) **Reduction:** Complete loss of bounded oxygen fractions. Examples: CuO + H2 → Cu + H2O and ZnO + C → Zn + CO.

Q17: A shiny brown coloured element 'X' on heating in air becomes black in colour. Name the element 'X' and the black coloured compound formed.

Answer: Element 'X' is Copper (Cu). The resulting black substance is Copper(II) Oxide (CuO).

Q18: Why do we apply paint on iron articles?

Answer: Paint seals the raw iron surface away from moisture and ambient atmospheric oxygen, preventing the chemical reaction that forms rust.

Q19: Oil and fat containing food items are flushed with nitrogen. Why?

Answer: Nitrogen gas creates an unreactive, oxygen-free atmosphere inside packaging. This blocks oxidation pathways and prevents food items from turning rancid.

Q20: Explain the following terms with one example each: (a) Corrosion (b) Rancidity

Answer:
(a) **Corrosion:** The gradual structural degradation of metals caused by environmental factors (e.g., the green film that forms on copper surfaces).
(b) **Rancidity:** The process where fats and oils oxidize over time, spoiling their smell and flavor profile (e.g., stale potato chips changing taste).
