Author: G. L. Fuentes

Theme: Faith, Hope, Nature, Human Kindness and Irony

Connect with the Chapter

🌾 Farmer’s Life

Farmers depend on land, rain and crops. One natural disaster can affect the whole family.

For Lencho, rain means a good harvest, and a good harvest means food and money.

The story asks us to think about faith in God and trust in good human beings.

  • Can faith give courage in difficult situations?
  • Should we also trust helpful people around us?

1. Before Filling the Money Order Form

NCERT first asks students to understand how money is sent through a post office and to discuss words such as counter, counter clerk, appropriate, acknowledgement, counterfoil and record.
WordMeaning
CounterA service desk in a post office or bank.
Counter clerkThe person who receives forms and money at the counter.
AppropriateSuitable or correct for a particular purpose.
AcknowledgementA written proof that money or a document has been received.
CounterfoilA part of a form or receipt kept as a record.
RecordOfficial written information kept for future reference.

4. NCERT Activity Questions — Solved

After studying the money order form, NCERT asks students to complete statements about who signs the form, who receives acknowledgement and how the communication space is used.
(i) In addition to the sender, the form has to be signed by the receiver / payee.
(ii) The Acknowledgement section of the form is sent back by the post office to the sender after the receiver / payee signs it.
(iii) The Space for Communication section is used for writing a short message from the sender to the receiver.
(iv) The form has six sections. The sender needs to fill out the main money order details and communication sections, while the receiver signs the acknowledgement/receipt section after receiving the money.

Visualizing Section 1: Lencho's solitary house perched on the crest of a low hill overlooking his fields.
Line 1: THE house — the only one in the entire valley — sat on the crest of a low hill.
ઘર — સમગ્ર ખીણમાં એકમાત્ર — એક નીચી ટેકરીની ટોચ પર આવેલું હતું.
Teacher Insight: Crest: Highest point of a hill. Establishes Lencho's physical isolation.
Line 2: From this height one could see the river and the field of ripe corn dotted with the flowers that always promised a good harvest.
આ ઊંચાઈ પરથી કોઈ પણ નદી અને પાકેલા મકાઈના ખેતરને જોઈ શકતું હતું, જે એવા ફૂલોથી ઘેરાયેલું હતું જે હંમેશા સારા પાકની ખાતરી આપતા હતા.
Line 3: The only thing the earth needed was a downpour or at least a shower.
પૃથ્વીને માત્ર એક ભારે વરસાદ અથવા ઓછામાં ઓછા એક હળવા વરસાદના ઝાપટાની જરૂર હતી.
Synonyms: Downpour: Heavy rain | Shower: Light rain.
Line 4: Throughout the morning Lencho — who knew his fields intimately — had done nothing else but see the sky towards the north-east.
આખી સવાર લેન્ચોએ — જે તેના ખેતરોને ખૂબ નજીકથી ઓળખતો હતો — ઉત્તર-પૂર્વ તરફના આકાશને જોવા સિવાય બીજું કંઈ કર્યું ન હતું.
Visualizing Section 2: Rain clouds rolling over the valley fields.
Line 5: “Now we’re really going to get some water, woman.”
"હવે આપણને ખરેખર થોડું પાણી મળવાનું છે, સ્ત્રી (પત્ની)."
Line 6: The woman, who was preparing supper, replied, “Yes, God willing.”
સ્ત્રી, જે સાંજનું ભોજન બનાવી રહી હતી, તેણે જવાબ આપ્યો, "હા, જો ભગવાનની ઈચ્છા હશે તો."
Vocabulary: Supper: Evening meal.
Line 7: The older boys were working in the field, while the smaller ones were playing near the house until the woman called to them all, “Come for dinner.”
મોટા છોકરાઓ ખેતરમાં કામ કરી રહ્યા હતા, જ્યારે નાના છોકરાઓ ઘરની નજીક રમી રહ્યા હતા, ત્યાં સુધી કે સ્ત્રીએ તે બધાને બૂમ પાડી, "જમવા માટે આવી જાઓ."
Line 8: It was during the meal that, just as Lencho had predicted, big drops of rain began to fall.
ભોજન દરમિયાન જ, લેન્ચોએ ભવિષ્યવાણી કરી હતી તેમ જ, વરસાદના મોટા ટીપાં પડવા લાગ્યા.
Line 9: In the north-east huge mountains of clouds could be seen approaching.
उत्तर-પૂર્વમાં વાદળોના વિશાળ પર્વતો આવતા જોઈ શકાતા હતા.
Metaphor Clouds are directly compared to massive mountains due to their volume.
Line 10: The air was fresh and sweet.
હવા તાજી અને મધુર હતી.
Line 11: The man went out for no other reason than to have the pleasure of feeling the rain on his body, and when he returned he exclaimed...
તે માણસ પોતાના શરીર પર વરસાદ અનુભવવાનો આનંદ માણવા સિવાય બીજા કોઈ કારણ વગર બહાર ગયો, અને જ્યારે તે પાછો ફર્યો ત્યારે તે આશ્ચર્યથી બોલી ઉઠ્યો...
Line 12: “These aren’t raindrops falling from the sky, they are new coins. The big drops are ten cent pieces and the little ones are fives.”
"આ આકાશમાંથી પડી રહેલા વરસાદના ટીપાં નથી, તેઓ નવા સિક્કા છે. મોટા ટીપાં દસ સેન્ટના સિક્કા છે અને નાના ટીપાં પાંચના છે."
Extended Metaphor Raindrops = New Coins. Rain guarantees a successful harvest bringing direct economic wealth.
Visualizing Section 3: The violent hailstorm stripping the landscape white.
Line 13: With a satisfied expression he regarded the field of ripe corn with its flowers, draped in a curtain of rain.
એક સંતોષકારક ભાવ સાથે તેણે તેના ફૂલોવાળા પાકેલા મકાઈના ખેતરને જોયું, જે વરસાદની ચાદર (પડદા) માં લપેટાયેલું હતું.
Metaphor "Curtain of rain" compares sheets of falling rain to a hanging cloth layer.
Line 14: But suddenly a strong wind began to blow and along with the rain very large hailstones began to fall.
પરંતુ અચાનક જોરદાર પવન ફૂંકાવા લાગ્યો અને વરસાદની સાથે ખૂબ મોટા કરા પડવા લાગ્યા.
Line 15: These truly did resemble new silver coins.
આ કરા ખરેખર નવા ચાંદીના સિક્કા જેવા દેખાતા હતા.
Irony Shiny white frozen ice elements look like silver coins but represent financial loss.
Line 16: The boys, exposing themselves to the rain, ran out to collect the frozen pearls.
છોકરાઓ, વરસાદમાં બહાર ખુલ્લા આવીને, થીજી ગયેલા મોતી (કરા) એકઠા કરવા બહાર દોડી ગયા.
Metaphor "Frozen pearls" compares round, cold white hailstones to precious sea jewels.
Line 17: “It’s really getting bad now,” exclaimed the man. “I hope it passes quickly.”
"હવે ખરેખર પરિસ્થિતિ બગડી રહી છે," લેન્ચોએ ચિંતાથી કહ્યું. "હું આશા રાખું છું કે આ ઝડપથી પસાર થઈ જાય."
Line 18: It did not pass quickly.
તે ઝડપથી પસાર ન થયું.
Line 19: For an hour the hail rained on the house, the garden, the hillside, the cornfield, on the whole valley.
એક કલાક સુધી ઘર પર, બગીચા પર, ટેકરીની બાજુ પર, મકાઈના ખેતરમાં, આખી ખીણ પર કરા વરસતા રહ્યા.
Line 20: The field was white, as if covered with salt.
ખેતર આખું સફેદ થઈ ગયું હતું, જાણે મીઠાથી ઢંકાયેલું ન હોય!
Simile "as if covered with salt" emphasizes the barrenness left behind by the storm.
Line 21: Not a leaf remained on the trees.
વૃક્ષો પર એક પણ પાંદડું બચ્યું ન હતું.
Line 22: The corn was totally destroyed.
મકાઈનો પાક સંપૂર્ણપણે નાશ પામ્યો હતો.
Line 23: The flowers were gone from the plants.
છોડ પરથી ફૂલો જતા રહ્યા હતા (ખરી પડ્યા હતા).
Line 24: Lencho’s soul was filled with sadness.
લેન્ચોનો આત્મા ઉદાસીથી ભરાઈ ગયો.
Line 25: When the storm had passed, he stood in the middle of the field and said to his sons...
જ્યારે વાવાઝોડું પસાર થઈ ગયું, ત્યારે તે ખેતરની વચ્ચે ઊભો રહ્યો અને તેણે તેના પુત્રોને કહ્યું...
Line 26: “A plague of locusts would have left more than this.”
"તીડનું આક્રમણ (ટોળું) પણ આના કરતાં વધુ છોડી ગયું હોત."
Core Concept: Locusts: Large insects traveling in big swarms that eat crops (તીડ). A biological pest leaves a baseline behind, whereas climate elements leave nothing.
Line 27: “The hail has left nothing.”
"કરાએ કંઈ બાકી રાખ્યું નથી."
Line 28: “This year we will have no corn.”
"આ વર્ષે આપણી પાસે કોઈ મકાઈ નહીં હોય."
Line 29: That night was a sorrowful one.
તે રાત્રિ ખૂબ જ દુઃખદાયક (શૉકભરી) હતી.
Line 30: “All our work, for nothing.”
"આપણી બધી જ મહેનત નકામી ગઈ."
Line 31: “There’s no one who can help us.”
"એવું કોઈ નથી જે આપણી મદદ કરી શકે."
Line 32: “We’ll all go hungry this year.”
"આ વર્ષે આપણે બધા ભૂખ્યા રહીશું."

📝 Oral Comprehension Check

1. What did Lencho hope for?
Lencho hoped for a downpour or at least a shower of rain so that his field of ripe corn would give a good harvest.
લેન્ચોએ સારા પાક માટે ભારે વરસાદ અથવા ઓછામાં ઓછા વરસાદના ઝાપટાની આશા રાખી હતી.
2. Why did Lencho say the raindrops were like “new coins”?
Lencho called the raindrops “new coins” because the rain would help his crop grow and bring him money after a good harvest.
વરસાદ સારો પાક આપશે અને પાકથી પૈસા મળશે, તેથી લેન્ચોએ વરસાદના ટીપાંને “નવા સિક્કા” કહ્યા.
3. How did the rain change? What happened to Lencho’s fields?
The rain changed into a hailstorm with strong wind and large hailstones. Lencho’s cornfield was completely destroyed.
વરસાદ જોરદાર પવન અને મોટા કરા સાથે કરાવૃષ્ટિમાં બદલાયો. લેન્ચોનું મકાઈનું ખેતર સંપૂર્ણ નાશ પામ્યું.
4. What were Lencho’s feelings when the hail stopped?
Lencho was filled with sadness and despair because the hail had destroyed everything and he feared his family would go hungry.
કરાવૃષ્ટિ બંધ થયા પછી લેન્ચો ખૂબ દુઃખી અને નિરાશ થયો, કારણ કે બધો પાક નાશ પામ્યો હતો.
Line 33: But in the hearts of all who lived in that solitary house in the middle of the valley, there was a single hope: help from God.
પરંતુ ખીણની વચ્ચે આવેલા તે એકાંત ઘરમાં રહેતા તમામ લોકોના હૃદયમાં એકમાત્ર આશા હતી: ભગવાન તરફથી મદદ.
Vocabulary: Solitary: Isolated, single (એકાંત).
Line 34: “Don’t be so upset, even though this seems like a total loss.”
"ભલે આ બધું જ ગુમાવી દીધું હોય એવું લાગે, છતાં આટલા બધા પરેશાન (ઉદાસ) ન થાઓ."
Line 35: “Remember, no one dies of hunger.”
"યાદ રાખો, ભૂખથી કોઈ મરતું નથી."
Line 36: “That’s what they say: no one dies of hunger.”
"લોકો આવું જ કહે છે: ભૂખથી કોઈ મરતું નથી."
Line 37: All through the night, Lencho thought only of his one hope: the help of God, whose eyes, as he had been instructed, see everything, even what is deep in one’s conscience.
આખી રાત દરમિયાન, લેન્ચોએ માત્ર પોતાની એકમાત્ર આશા વિશે જ વિચાર્યું: ભગવાનની મદદ, જેમ કે તેને શીખવવામાં આવ્યું હતું કે ભગવાનની આંખો બધું જ જુએ છે, માણસના અંતરાત્માના ઊંડાણમાં શું છે તે પણ.
Vocabulary: Conscience: Inner moral filter of right and wrong (અંતરાત્મા).
Line 38: Lencho was an ox of a man, working like an animal in the fields, but still he knew how to write.
લેન્ચો એક બળદ જેવો માણસ હતો, ખેતરોમાં પ્રાણીની જેમ સખત મજૂરી કરતો હતો, છતાં તે જાણતો હતો કે કેવી રીતે લખવું.
Metaphor Ox of a man: Expresses manual endurance. He can perform exhausting work, yet remains literate.
Line 39: The following Sunday, at daybreak, he began to write a letter which he himself would carry to town and place in the mail.
પછીના રવિવારે, પરોઢિયે (સવારના સમયે), તેણે એક પત્ર લખવાનું શરૂ કર્યું જે તે પોતે શહેરમાં લઈ જશે અને ટપાલમાં નાખશે.
Line 40: It was nothing less than a letter to God.
તે ભગવાનને લખેલા પત્રથી કંઈ ઓછું ન હતું.
Visualizing Section 6: Dropping the mail into the town sorting box.
Line 41: God, he wrote, “if you don’t help me, my family and I will go hungry this year.”
ભગવાન, તેણે લખ્યું, "જો તમે મને મદદ નહીં કરો, તો મારો પરિવાર અને હું આ વર્ષે ભૂખ્યા રહીશું."
Line 42: “I need a hundred pesos in order to sow my field again and to live until the crop comes, because the hailstorm...”
"મારે મારા ખેતરમાં ફરીથી વાવણી કરવા માટે અને નવો પાક ન આવે ત્યાં સુધી જીવવા માટે સો પેસોની જરૂર છે, કારણ કે આ કરાના વાવાઝોડાએ..."
Context: Pesos: Currency (પેસો) | Sow: To plant seeds (વાવણી કરવી).
Line 43: He wrote ‘To God’ on the envelope, put the letter inside and, still troubled, went to town.
તેણે પરબિડીયા (કવર) પર 'ભગવાનને' (To God) એવું સરનામું લખ્યું, પત્ર અંદર મૂક્યો અને, હજી પણ ચિંતિત અવસ્થામાં, શહેર તરફ ગયો.
Line 44: At the post office, he placed a stamp on the letter and dropped it into the mailbox.
પોસ્ટ ઓફિસે પહોંચીને, તેણે પત્ર પર ટિકિટ લગાવી અને તેને ટપાલની પેટીમાં નાખી દીધો.
Line 45: One of the employees, who was a postman and also helped at the post office, went to his boss laughing heartily and showed him the letter to God.

👤 Lencho

Lencho is a hardworking farmer. He is simple, honest and deeply faithful. He believes God sees everything and can help him in difficulty.

Exam Point: His strength is faith; his weakness is blind distrust of people.

👨‍💼 The Postmaster

The postmaster is kind, sympathetic and generous. He respects Lencho’s faith and collects money to help him.

Exam Point: He represents human kindness and compassion.

📬 Post Office Employees

The employees first laugh at the letter, but later help Lencho by contributing money. They are cooperative and charitable.

Exam Point: They are the real helpers, though Lencho calls them crooks.

📚 Thinking About the Text

Instruction: These are the textbook questions from Thinking About the Text. Click Show Answer to study the solution.

📖 Question 1

Who does Lencho have complete faith in? Which sentences in the story tell you this?

Lencho has complete faith in God. He believes that God sees everything and can help him in his difficulty.

The sentences that show this are: “there was a single hope: help from God” and “God,” he wrote, “if you don’t help me, my family and I will go hungry this year.”

💡 Exam Point: This answer shows Lencho’s deep, unquestioning faith in God.

📖 Question 2

Why does the postmaster send money to Lencho? Why does he sign the letter ‘God’?

The postmaster sends money to Lencho because he is deeply impressed by Lencho’s faith. He does not want Lencho’s faith in God to be shaken.

He signs the letter “God” because he wants Lencho to believe that the help has come from God himself.

💡 Exam Point: The postmaster is kind, sensitive and respectful of Lencho’s faith.

📖 Question 3

Did Lencho try to find out who had sent the money to him? Why/Why not?

No, Lencho did not try to find out who had sent the money to him.

He was so confident in his faith that he believed the money had been sent by God. He showed no surprise when he saw the money because he expected God to help him.

💡 Exam Point: Lencho’s reaction proves the strength of his faith, but also shows his simplicity and innocence.

📖 Question 4

Who does Lencho think has taken the rest of the money? What is the irony in the situation?

Lencho thinks that the post office employees have taken the rest of the money.

The irony is that the post office employees and the postmaster were the very people who collected and sent money to help him. Instead of being grateful to them, Lencho calls them “a bunch of crooks.”

💡 Exam Point: This is situational irony because the reality is opposite to what Lencho believes.

📖 Question 5

Are there people like Lencho in the real world? What kind of a person would you say he is?

Yes, there are people like Lencho in the real world. Such people are simple, hardworking and deeply faithful. They may trust God completely but may not understand the kindness of people around them.

Lencho can be described as naive, unquestioning, hardworking and faithful. He is not greedy because he asks only for the amount he needs to sow his field again and support his family.

💡 Exam Point: Use words like naive, unquestioning, hardworking and faithful to describe Lencho.

📖 Question 6

There are two kinds of conflict in the story: between humans and nature, and between humans themselves. How are these conflicts illustrated?

The conflict between humans and nature is shown when the hailstorm destroys Lencho’s entire crop. Lencho works hard in his field, but nature ruins his efforts in a short time.

The conflict between humans themselves is shown through Lencho’s distrust of the post office employees. They help him by collecting money, but Lencho believes they have stolen the remaining thirty pesos.

💡 Exam Point: The story shows both the destructive power of nature and the misunderstanding that can arise between human beings.

🌟 Value-Based Questions

We learn that faith gives courage in difficult situations. However, faith should also be balanced with gratitude and trust in good human beings.
Q2. Why should we help people in need?
Helping others shows humanity. The postmaster and his employees helped Lencho without expecting anything in return.
Q3. Was the postmaster a role model? Explain.
Yes. He respected Lencho’s faith and took practical action to help him. His kindness teaches us compassion and generosity.

Lencho was a simple farmer who had immense faith in God. After a severe hailstorm destroyed his entire crop, he faced the danger of starvation. However, when Lencho received only seventy pesos, he believed that God could never make a mistake. ❄️ Dust of Snow

Poet: Robert Frost

Theme: A small natural moment can change our mood.

Dust of Snow

Robert Frost
The way a crow
Shook down on me
The dust of snow
From a hemlock tree

Has given my heart
A change of mood
And saved some part
Of a day I had rued.

Simple Explanation: A small natural incident changed the poet’s sad mood and saved part of his day.
Q1. What is a “dust of snow”? What does the poet say has changed his mood? How has the poet’s mood changed?
“Dust of snow” means the fine particles of snow. The poet says that the dust of snow falling on him from a hemlock tree changed his mood. His mood changed from sadness and regret to freshness and positivity.
Q2. How does Frost present nature in this poem?
Robert Frost presents nature in an unusual way. He does not use traditionally beautiful objects like flowers, birds or sunshine. Instead, he uses a crow, a hemlock tree and snow. These simple and even gloomy natural objects bring a positive change in the poet’s mood.
(i) What birds are usually named in poems? Is a crow often mentioned? What image comes to mind?
Birds like nightingales, skylarks, cuckoos and doves are usually named in poems. A crow is not usually used as a beautiful poetic bird. It often suggests harsh sound, black colour, commonness or sadness.
(ii) What is a hemlock tree? Why doesn’t the poet write about a more beautiful tree?
A hemlock is a poisonous tree. The poet chooses it instead of a beautiful tree to show that even ordinary or negative-looking things in nature can bring joy and change.
(iii) What do the crow and hemlock represent? What does the dust of snow stand for?
The crow and hemlock generally represent sorrow, sadness and negativity. The dust of snow stands for a small, pure and refreshing natural moment that changes the poet’s mood.
What are Poetic Devices?
Poetic devices are special techniques used by poets to make a poem more beautiful, meaningful and expressive. They help create sound, imagery, rhythm and deeper meaning.
1. Alliteration
Repetition of the same consonant sound.
Example: “Has given my heart” — the /h/ sound is repeated.
2. Symbolism
When an object represents a deeper idea.
Crow and hemlock symbolize sadness or negativity. Dust of snow symbolizes a refreshing change.
3. Imagery
Words that create a picture in the reader’s mind.
The image of a crow shaking snow from a hemlock tree is clear and visual.
4. Contrast
Opposite ideas placed together.
Negative symbols like crow and hemlock create a positive change in the poet’s mood.
5. Rhyme Scheme
The pattern of rhyming words at the end of lines.
The rhyme scheme is abab cdcd.

🔥 Fire and Ice

Poet: Robert Frost

Theme: Desire and hatred can both destroy.

Fire and Ice

Robert Frost
Some say the world will end in fire,
Some say in ice.
From what I’ve tasted of desire
I hold with those who favour fire.

But if it had to perish twice,
I think I know enough of hate
To say that for destruction ice
Is also great
And would suffice.
Glossary:
Fire — desire, greed, passion Ice — hatred, coldness, cruelty Perish — die / be destroyed Desire — strong wish or greed Suffice — be enough
Simple Explanation: The poet says that some people believe the world will end because of fire, while others believe it will end because of ice. Here, fire stands for desire, greed and uncontrolled passion. Ice stands for hatred, coldness and cruelty. Robert Frost suggests that both desire and hatred are powerful enough to destroy the world.

Simple Explanation: Fire represents desire, greed and uncontrolled passion. Ice represents hatred, coldness and cruelty. Both can destroy the world.
Q1. There are many ideas about how the world will “end”. Do you think the world will end someday? Have you ever thought what would happen if the sun got so hot that it burst, or grew colder and colder?
This is an open-ended thinking question. Scientifically, the world may end someday because everything in the universe changes with time. If the sun became extremely hot, life on Earth would burn. If it became colder and colder, Earth would freeze. The poet uses these possibilities symbolically to discuss human desire and hatred.
Q2. For Frost, what do “fire” and “ice” stand for?
In the poem, “fire” stands for desire, greed, anger and uncontrolled passion. “Ice” stands for hatred, coldness, cruelty and lack of human feelings. Both are destructive.
Q3. What is the rhyme scheme of the poem? How does it help in bringing out the contrasting ideas in the poem?
The rhyme scheme is aba abc bcb. The short and sharp rhyme pattern makes the poem powerful and memorable. It also helps present two contrasting ideas — fire and ice — in a compact way.

3. Alliteration

Burning fire and freezing ice create strong visual images.
